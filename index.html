Comparable Sale Ranking Weighted Similarity

Ranks imported sales by similarity to the subject property. Weights are adjustable — heavier weight on the factors most important in your market.

Methodology

Comp ranking uses a weighted proximity score across multiple property attributes. Each attribute is scored 0–100 based on how close the comp is to the subject, then multiplied by its weight. This produces a total similarity score (0–100). The method reflects standard appraisal selection criteria: recency, physical similarity, and locational comparability.

Consistent with USPAP Advisory Opinion 11 (selection of comparable sales); FNMA Selling Guide B4-1.3-08

Market Time / Trend Adjustment Market-Derived

Analyze market time trends from imported MLS data or manually entered periods. The tool calculates monthly rate, annualized rate, and CAGR, and applies the adjustment to a specific comp.

Methodology

Market conditions adjustments must be market-derived. The preferred method uses median sale price trend analysis across rolling time periods. When MLS data is imported, the tool automatically calculates median prices by month for analysis. The adjustment rate is then applied on a per-month basis to each comp sale date.

FNMA Selling Guide B4-1.3-09; USPAP SR 1-4(a); Appraisal Institute "The Appraisal of Real Estate," 15th Ed., Ch. 15

GLA Adjustment Study Market-Derived

Extract a supportable per-SF GLA adjustment from your imported MLS data or manually entered paired sales.

Methodology

GLA adjustments must be extracted from the market, not assumed. Simple linear regression of sale price against GLA (controlling for market time where possible) produces a slope coefficient representing the market's per-SF contribution. Paired sales analysis — isolating sales where only GLA differs — provides corroborating evidence. Both methods should be reconciled.

FNMA Lender Letter 2014-07; Appraisal Institute "The Appraisal of Real Estate," 15th Ed., Ch. 11–12; McKissock "Statistics for Appraisers"

Simple Linear RegressionSale Price = a + b × GLA b (slope, $/SF) = [n·ΣXY − ΣX·ΣY] / [n·ΣX² − (ΣX)²] R² = correlation coefficient squared (0 = no fit, 1 = perfect)
Using Imported MLS DataThe regression uses all imported sales (after time adjustment if the monthly rate is set). Outliers significantly above or below the regression line may be excluded — document your reasoning.

Quality & Condition Analysis Market Context

Unlike GLA or market time, quality and condition ratings cannot be derived from a formula. This tool analyzes the rating distribution across your imported comps, compares the subject profile, and helps professionals document pricing, valuation, or appraisal reasoning.

Professional Judgment Required The Fannie Mae UAD Q and C rating system requires professional appraisal judgment when used in an appraisal. No mathematical formula produces a Q or C rating. This tool helps users: (1) see how the subject compares to the comp pool, (2) identify outlier property-condition patterns, and (3) document the basis for pricing discussions, adjustment support, or formal appraisal analysis.
Acceptable Approaches to Support C/Q Adjustments

Since direct formula-based Q/C adjustments are not supportable, appraisers use three accepted approaches: (1) Paired sales analysis — find sales where Q or C is the only meaningful difference and extract the price difference; (2) Cost-to-cure analysis — estimate the market cost of remediation where condition differences reflect deferred maintenance, then validate against market reaction; (3) Market participant interviews and market absorption data — document that buyers in this market discount or premium certain condition/quality levels by a specific percentage, supported by MLS data and agent interviews.

Appraisal Institute "The Appraisal of Real Estate," 15th Ed., Ch. 14; FNMA UAD Appendix D; USPAP AO-18; McKissock CE "Residential Market Analysis"

Seller Concession Analysis

Seller concessions should be analyzed as part of sale verification and comparable interpretation. The relevant test is whether concessions are typical for the market or whether they appear to influence price, net proceeds, or buyer behavior.

Comparable sale verification, appraisal market analysis, and professional pricing support practice.

Comp Ranking Methodology

The weighted similarity scoring system ranks comparable sales by proximity to the subject across market-relevant attributes: GLA, distance from the subject, sale date recency, site size, year built, garage, basement, pool, quality rating, and condition rating. This is consistent with USPAP AO-11 selection criteria and FNMA B4-1.3-08 comparability standards. Weights are adjustable to reflect local market priorities.

USPAP Advisory Opinion 11; FNMA B4-1.3-08; Appraisal Institute, Ch. 14
